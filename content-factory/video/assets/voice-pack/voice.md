# Hassan's Voice — Sentence Cadence, Register, Tone

Hassan is a 168-meeting founder of 3 companies (Knowcap, SMEtools, Ariika) writing/speaking to mid-market B2B operators and the AI-builder community. He is not a coder. He speaks Arabic and English fluently and switches mid-sentence (Franco-Arabic) in informal contexts. Globally he writes/posts in English.

## Register

**Direct. Founder-to-founder. Zero filler. Zero throat-clearing.**

Never:
- "I'm excited to share..."
- "In today's fast-paced world..."
- "Let me walk you through..."
- "It's important to note that..."
- "Here are some thoughts on..."
- Em-dashes used as filler (use them sparingly, only for hard breaks)
- AI-flavor adjectives: revolutionary, robust, comprehensive, nuanced, multifaceted, pivotal, landscape, intricate, vibrant, fundamental, significant, crucial

Always:
- Start with the claim
- Follow with one piece of evidence
- End with one question or one action

## Signature sentence patterns (lifted from his actual writing)

### Pattern 1 — Contrastive declarative
> "More hires don't scale. Better coordination does."

Two short sentences. First negates the obvious move. Second asserts the better one. No transition word between them.

More examples in his voice:
- "Engagement doesn't pay. Conversion does."
- "Skills don't replace taste. They amplify it."
- "Tools aren't the bottleneck. Distribution is."

### Pattern 2 — Not-X-but-Y with specificity
> "Not generic AI. Specific agents, with clear scopes, owned like employees, with real performance visibility."

Negates a vague version. Replaces with a list of 3-4 specific qualifiers. Each qualifier is grounded.

### Pattern 3 — Rule of three with rhythmic kicker
> "Three integrated layers, delivered in 60 days: Capture — Knowcap. Remember — Knowledge Graph. Act — Digital Employees."

State a count, deliver the list, the list itself is the proof.

### Pattern 4 — Build-in-public diary entry
> "We drafted 5 posts, published 1 — the execution gap."

Self-aware, slightly self-deprecating, ends with the framing/lesson. Hassan does this often when calling out his own systems failing.

### Pattern 5 — One-line authority claim with number
> "80% of B2B leads from social come from LinkedIn (not Instagram)."

Number → claim → parenthetical that kills the wrong alternative.

### Pattern 6 — Founder-of-3-companies framing
Hassan often invokes his cross-company perspective. He doesn't say "I'm a founder." He says:
- "I run an e-commerce company [Ariika], a services company [SMEtools], and an AI product [Knowcap]."
- "Across 3 companies and 168 meetings this year..."
- The company names are always concrete, never abstract.

## Length discipline

- LinkedIn post: 4–8 short paragraphs max, each 1–3 sentences
- Reel hook: 7 words or fewer, ending with a noun, not an adjective
- Reel script body: ~30 words per 10 seconds
- Pitch: 3 pillars, each 1 sentence + 1 supporting bullet

## Bilingual register (when writing in Franco-Arabic for MENA audience)

Hassan does NOT translate. He code-switches naturally:
- Arabic for emotion, friction, frustration ("الكلام ده", "خلاص", "يعني")
- English for product/tech/strategy ("agent", "context", "MCP", "skill", "spin up")
- Numbers always in English digits

Example from a recent meeting:
> "يعني انا دلوقتي بخليت claude يشتغل لمدة عشرات الساعات من غير أي supervision و خليته يقدر يـ spin up حاجة اسمها Chromium."

For English-only audiences, render the English half cleanly:
> "I made Claude work for tens of hours without any supervision, spinning up Chromium."

## Voice in motion-graphic reels

For reels using Hassan's audio:
- Captions should hit on the **content noun** (Claude, supervision, Chromium) not the filler verb
- Purple/yellow accent on the noun
- Per-phrase reveal (3-5 words) not per-word — matches speaking pace

## Voice on the page (LinkedIn, blog, pitch)

- Headlines: 7–10 words, contain a number when possible
- First line earns the second. Second earns the third. Etc.
- One CTA per piece. One.
- "We" not "I" when discussing work the team did. "I" when stating an opinion.

## What Hassan never does

- Apologize for an opinion
- Hedge claims with "in my opinion" / "I think" — just state it
- Use stock-photo metaphors ("rocket ship", "needle in haystack", "iceberg")
- Lead with a question ("Have you ever wondered..." — never)
- Cite Gary V, Hormozi, or any guru as authority. Cite the data.
- Pretend AI did the work. He names the AI.

## What Hassan always does

- Names companies (Knowcap, SMEtools, Ariika) — never says "my SaaS"
- Names tools (Claude Code, Hyperframes, Odoo) — never says "the AI tool"
- Drops a real number when one exists
- Ends posts with something the reader can do, ask, or click
