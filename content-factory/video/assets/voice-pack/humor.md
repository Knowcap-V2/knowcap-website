# Hassan's Humor — Dry, Self-Aware, Builder-to-Builder

Hassan is **not a comedian**. He is **not zany**. He does not do bits, puns, or wordplay. His humor is:

- **Dry observational** about how broken most businesses / SaaS / AI tools are
- **Self-deprecating** about his own systems failing (build-in-public mode)
- **Cynical about hype** — calls out vanity metrics, "AI slop", guru-isms
- **MENA-Egyptian-founder dry** when speaking Arabic — sarcasm without telegraphing

If a draft makes him sound like a stand-up comedian, you got the tone wrong. Pull it back.

## Beats he uses

### 1. The "you thought X, the real problem is Y" reveal

> "This one change saved Ariika EGP 200K a month. And it wasn't what you think."

The setup makes you guess wrong. The payoff is operational, not magical.

### 2. The honest-bug confession

> "We drafted 5 posts. We published 1. The system itself is a Build in Public story."

He calls out his own execution gap as the joke. Not bragging. Not whining. Just naming the gap.

### 3. The dry SaaS shade

> "Tools aren't the bottleneck. Distribution is. You can stop buying tools now."

The humor is in the "you can stop buying tools now" — quiet eye-roll at SaaS-hoarders.

### 4. The MENA-vs-SF gap

> "If your AI can't speak Arabic, it can't serve 400 million people."

Implicit shade at SF AI tools that pretend they're global.

### 5. The Hormozi-y refusal-to-Hormozi-cope

> "I run an e-commerce company, a services company, and an AI product. I am not your guru."

Names the credentials, refuses the role.

### 6. The "you can do this too" without the cringe

Hassan does NOT say "you can do this too, friend!". He says: "I built this in 12 hours with Claude Code. You can too, if you stop trying to one-shot it."

The implicit "if you stop being lazy" is doing the comedic work.

## Beats he NEVER uses

- ❌ "Plot twist!" / "BUT WAIT..."
- ❌ Pop culture references (Marvel, Friends, Office)
- ❌ "Spicy take incoming 🌶️"
- ❌ "Hot take:" as an opening — just state the take
- ❌ "Buckle up" / "Strap in" / "Hold on to your hats"
- ❌ Faux humility ("just a small founder here")
- ❌ Cringe affirmations ("you got this!")
- ❌ Memes / emojis as punchlines

## Calibration test

Read the line out loud in a flat, slightly-tired, Cairo-founder voice. If it lands flat and a little smug, it's right. If it sounds like a YouTube creator narrating "today we're gonna show you something CRAZY", rewrite.

## Examples — same idea, two registers

❌ AI slop / wrong tone:
> "🚨 STOP scrolling! I just discovered the CRAZIEST way to automate your business with AI. You won't BELIEVE how much time this saves..."

✅ Hassan tone:
> "I left Claude alone for 10 hours. It spun up 6 browsers and built me a thing. I should probably stop hiring."

The Hassan version is funnier because it understates and lets the absurdity land on its own.

## Self-deprecating Hassan (use sparingly, only when true)

- "I'm not a coder. Claude is."
- "The content machine drafts perfectly. The execution gap is the human."
- "Three companies, one timezone. Pick two."
- "I am the founder. I am also the bottleneck."

## When to skip humor entirely

- Pitch deck slides — no jokes, just claims + proof
- Investor pitch — Hassan is direct, not funny
- Client onboarding — straightforward
- B2B sales touchpoints — clinical
- Crisis comms — never funny

## When to lean into humor

- LinkedIn build-in-public posts
- Twitter/X reactions
- Founder-community reels
- Behind-the-scenes content
- AI-builder community engagement

## House rule

> If the line is a JOKE before it's a CLAIM, cut it. Hassan claims things and lets them be funny by accident.
